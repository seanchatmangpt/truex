# Summary: A Survey of Maturity Models from Nolan to DevOps

## 1. Thesis
This paper documents the lineage of process maturity from the foundational Statistical Process Control (SPC) of Walter Shewhart to contemporary DevOps frameworks. It argues that while maturity models have evolved from rigid, bureaucratic structures (CMM/CMMI) to flexible, domain-specific grids (Agile/DevOps), their core objective remains the systematic reduction of non-conformances through deterministic progression.

## 2. Key Findings
*   **Genealogy of Quality:** Modern maturity models are direct descendants of the "Plan-Do-Check-Act" (PDCA) cycle and Shewhart’s SPC, which established that quality is a function of process stability and incremental improvement.
*   **The Control Inversion:** Richard Nolan (1973) identified that as business complexity outpaces IT control, organizations must transition through stages of maturity to restore equilibrium.
*   **Capability vs. Compliance:** The shift from CMM to CMMI reflected a need for "integration" and flexibility (staged vs. continuous approaches), yet the industry increasingly favors "Lean" and "Agile" models that prioritize rapid feedback over heavy documentation.
*   **Specialization:** The proliferation of models (ITIL, MDM, Cybersecurity) demonstrates that "maturity" is not a monolithic state but a domain-specific capability that must be assessed against localized laws.

## 3. Truex Post-Gall Relevance: The Grammar of Consequence

*   **Substrate:** Where the paper describes "maturity" as a qualitative measurement of an organization's ability, Truex defines maturity as the physical stability of the Substrate itself. A "Level 5" process in Truex is one where the Substrate's laws are inviolable.
*   **Membrane & Attempt:** The paper’s "Managed" and "Defined" stages are implemented in Truex as the **Membrane**. Every "Attempt" at an operation is intercepted by this membrane, ensuring that only operations compliant with the defined maturity law are allowed to proceed.
*   **Admit & Refuse:** Traditional models rely on human "assessors" to check for non-conformances. Truex automates this: the membrane will **Admit** operations that satisfy the process law and **Refuse** those that deviate, providing zero-latency enforcement of the maturity level.
*   **Receipt:** The "Statistical Process Control" (SPC) of the future is the **Receipt**. Instead of sampling data to find non-conformances, Truex generates a cryptographic Receipt for every Consequence. This provides an absolute, proof-bearing audit trail that a process is operating at its intended maturity level, effectively ending the era of "probabilistic" quality management.
