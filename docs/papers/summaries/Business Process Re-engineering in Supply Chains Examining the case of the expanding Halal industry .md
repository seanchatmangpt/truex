# Summary: Business Process Re-engineering in Supply Chains (Halal Case Study)

## 1. Thesis
This paper proposes a formalized, automated supply chain framework to solve the crisis of authenticity in the global Halal industry. It argues that by re-engineering business processes using ebXML and CMMI maturity principles, organizations can transition from manual, error-prone inspections to a system of "drastic monitoring" where compliance is a real-time, automated invariant.

## 2. Key Findings
*   **The Authenticity Gap:** The Halal market is compromised by fake certificates, cross-contamination, and non-compliant processing (e.g., animal welfare violations).
*   **Process Re-engineering:** Success requires shifting from qualitative "checking" to quantitative "enforcement" across core processes: transportation, inventory, slaughtering, and production.
*   **Decentralized Responsibility:** Monitoring must not be the sole burden of a central certification body; instead, every actor in the supply chain must participate in a "Trading Partnership" that shares responsibility for auditability.
*   **Technology as Enforcer:** The use of XML-based data flows (ebXML) allows for the standardized exchange of health reports and certificates, significantly shortening the "certification cycle" while increasing causal certainty.
*   **Maturity Level 5:** The framework aims for the highest CMMI level, where process improvement is continuous and exceptions (non-conformances) are handled through deterministic logic rather than human intervention.
