# Summary: How to Manage Complex Programs
**File:** How to manage complex programs.pdf

## Main Thesis
The text argues that managing complexity at scale requires a departure from traditional linear project management toward a multi-dimensional orchestration of interlinked initiatives. It posits that success in complex programs is dependent on the rigorous management of interdependencies (interfaces), strategic alignment with organizational goals, and a governance structure that balances distributed leadership with centralized control.

## Key Findings
1.  **Dependency Orchestration:** The "Interface Closure Index" is introduced as a primary metric for tracking the fulfillment of cross-project dependencies. It calculates progress based on the ratio of completed interfaces to the total planned, weighted by timeline consumption.
2.  **Strategic Initiation:** Initiation of complex programs requires a "top-down" frame that includes technology choice evaluation via decision trees (weighing new vs. established tech) and alignment with process maturity models (CMM, CMMI, OPM3).
3.  **Release Waves:** Complexity is mitigated by organizing deliverables into "Release Waves" (integrated or incremental), which allow for the parallel development of derivative products while maintaining a stable core platform.
4.  **Requirement Prioritization:** The use of the Kano model and the categorization of requirements into "Now" vs. "Not Yet" ensure that high-value deliverables are prioritized while managing stakeholder expectations and scope creep.
5.  **Governance Domains:** Program management is divided into five critical domains: Strategy Alignment, Stakeholder Engagement, Benefits Management, Governance, and Life Cycle Management.
