# Self-Assessing the Capability of Usability Process

## Main Thesis
This paper introduces **UPCASE**, a lightweight self-assessment method designed specifically for small organizations to evaluate and improve their usability processes. Aligned with ISO/IEC 29110 and ISO/TR 18529, the method provides a simplified framework for non-experts to systematically benchmark their user-centered design capabilities.

## Key Findings
- **Simplified Reference Models:** UPCASE adapts complex international standards into a two-dimensional evaluation model (process and quality), focusing on technical engineering practices rather than high-level business strategy.
- **Consensus-Based Assessment:** The method employs "Assessment Poker" (modeled after Planning Poker) to ensure collective participation and mitigate the influence of dominant voices during the self-evaluation process.
- **Capability Gaps:** Case studies indicate that while small organizations are proficient at producing design solutions, they consistently underperform in evaluating those designs against stakeholder requirements.
- **Reliability and Usability:** Statistical analysis (Weighted Kappa and Cronbach's Alpha) confirms that the method is reliable and understandable for non-experts, making it an effective tool for continuous process improvement.

## Truex Relevance
The UPCASE framework aligns with the **Truex mandate for decentralized capability verification**. In the Truex substrate, autonomous sub-agents and organizations must verify their own operational integrity. The consensus-driven, low-overhead assessment model of UPCASE provides a protocol for verifying that human-centric interfaces within the substrate meet the required 'Grammar of Consequence' for usability and safety.
