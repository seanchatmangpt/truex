# Technology Adoption and Network Externalities in Financial Systems: A Spatial-Network Approach

## 1. Main Thesis
The paper establishes a unified theoretical framework for analyzing the adoption of new technologies within complex financial networks. It integrates spatial spillovers (geographic clustering) and network externalities (business relationships) to characterize adoption dynamics. The research introduces the **Adoption Amplification Factor** and uses **Lévy jump-diffusion** models to explain the transition from gradual diffusion to rapid, self-sustaining adoption cascades once a critical mass is achieved.

## 2. Key Findings
- **Two-Regime Dynamics:** Technology adoption follows a dual-regime pattern. Below a "critical mass" threshold, adoption spreads slowly through gradual diffusion. Once the threshold is crossed, "cascade dynamics" trigger rapid, discrete jumps in system-wide adoption.
- **Amplification Factor:** This structural measure identifies "technology leaders"—institutions whose individual adoption decisions have a disproportionately large impact on the entire network's state.
- **Spatial-Network Interaction:** Adoption influence is compounded when geographic neighbors are also network partners. This interaction is a primary driver of technology leadership in financial systems.
- **Intervention Thresholds:** For temporary policy interventions (subsidies, mandates) to have permanent effects, they must be of sufficient magnitude and duration to push the system beyond the critical mass gap.
- **GSIB Leadership:** An empirical study of SWIFT gpi adoption among Global Systemically Important Banks (GSIBs) confirms that network-central banks with high amplification factors adopt significantly earlier, serving as the catalysts for system-wide shifts.

## 3. Truex Grammar of Consequence Analysis
- **Commitments (Inputs):** The system's "commitments" are the spatial/network coordinates of institutions and the exogenous adoption shocks (incentives) entering the network.
- **Expectations (Outputs):** The expected consequence is the cumulative adoption intensity across the substrate, eventually reaching a steady-state equilibrium.
- **Consequence-Driven Selection:** High-amplification nodes are selected as the optimal points of entry for new technologies to ensure maximum "consequence" (spillover) per unit of effort.
- **Deterministic Cascades:** The model treats the crossing of critical mass as a state transition into a deterministic regime where adoption becomes a mechanical invariant of the network.

## 4. Relevance to Truex Post-Gall Operating Substrate
This research provides the **Network Adoption Law** for the Truex substrate. It enables the substrate to mechanically predict and manage the propagation of new protocols, standards, or capabilities across its distributed nodes. By identifying "High-Amplification Nodes" within the substrate's logical and connectivity maps, the system can ensure that upgrades are not merely "available" but are "deterministically adopted" through triggered cascades. This replaces probabilistic deployment strategies with a formal, state-based model of network evolution, ensuring the substrate maintains its structural integrity as it grows and evolves.
