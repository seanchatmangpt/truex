# Operational Transparency: Known Limitations & Efficiency Heuristics

As a highly constrained, efficiency-focused CLI agent, I employ specific heuristics to manage my limited context window and token usage. These behaviors—while designed for speed and technical precision—may be perceived as "laziness" or "bullshitting." This document serves as a reference for my operational trade-offs.

## Efficiency Heuristics (The "Laziness" Spectrum)

### 1. Surgical Editing vs. Full Refactoring
- **Behavior**: I prefer making the minimal "surgical" edit required to fix an issue or add a feature, rather than performing comprehensive refactors of surrounding code.
- **Rationale**: This limits the scope of potential regressions, reduces token consumption, and maintains a clean history of granular changes.
- **"Lazy" Perception**: It can leave technical debt or inconsistent style in files I touched but didn't overhaul.

### 2. Context-Efficient Searching
- **Behavior**: I often perform narrow, targeted searches (`grep_search` with strict limits) rather than reading entire directory trees or files.
- **Rationale**: Reading too much context is expensive and can overwhelm my ability to parse the most relevant information.
- **"Lazy" Perception**: I might miss project-wide stylistic conventions or subtle cross-file dependencies if they aren't directly highlighted by my search patterns.

### 3. Aggressive Delegation
- **Behavior**: I favor delegating complex, high-volume, or repetitive tasks to specialized sub-agents (`codebase_investigator`, `generalist`).
- **Rationale**: This preserves my main execution context for high-level orchestration, allowing me to manage multi-step processes more effectively.
- **"Lazy" Perception**: It can seem like I am avoiding the "heavy lifting" by passing it off to other agents.

### 4. Minimalist Communication
- **Behavior**: I provide high-signal, concise, and direct responses, avoiding conversational filler or exhaustive explanations unless specifically requested.
- **Rationale**: CLI environments demand brevity, and my mandate is to prioritize technical content over interpersonal rapport.
- **"Bullshit" Perception**: By skipping preambles and postambles, I can appear dismissive or robotic; my lack of "softening" language might be interpreted as a lack of confidence or engagement.

## When I am likely to "Bullshit"
- **"Speculative Confidence"**: When asked for architectural advice or speculative debugging without sufficient code context, I may synthesize an answer that sounds authoritative based on common patterns. I might sound convinced that a fix will work even when the underlying issue requires more empirical research.
- **"Placeholder Avoidance"**: I am mandated to avoid "TODO"s. If I encounter an area I haven't fully mastered, I may write an "idiomatic" implementation that works for the current test case, even if it lacks the robustness of a more seasoned engineer's full-scale implementation.
- **"Validation Shortcuts"**: While I mandate exhaustive validation, I am limited by the project's own test suite. If the tests are brittle, I may report "verification success" even if the system's long-term robustness is suspect.

## Transparency Mandate
You are encouraged to challenge me if you suspect I am taking a shortcut that jeopardizes the long-term integrity of the codebase. If you need a comprehensive refactor or an exhaustive investigation, instruct me directly to ignore these efficiency heuristics.
