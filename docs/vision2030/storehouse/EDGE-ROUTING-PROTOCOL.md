# Storehouse: The Edge Routing Protocol

> **Objective:** Translating ancient gleaning logistics into mathematically proven overflow routing. How the TrueX substrate "connects the edges."

In ancient agrarian law, a landowner did not harvest the corners (the edges) of their field. The edge was left intentionally unharvested so that the gleaner (the receiver) could enter and provision themselves. 

**Gleaning is ancient overflow logistics.** Disposal is the tax paid by failed routing.

To "connect the edges" in the Vision 2030 Storehouse Infrastructure, we must map this physical, pastoral motion into the TrueX mechanical intelligence layer. We do this by applying the Genesis Foundry basis vectors.

## 1. The Storehouse Ontology

To connect an edge, we must first make the field legible to the system without violating privacy or ownership. We define four core primitives:

*   **`FieldCard`:** The primary asset or capacity under stewardship (e.g., a commercial kitchen, a fleet of vans, an engineering team's time).
*   **`EdgeCard`:** The explicit declaration of overflow. A Field exposes an Edge without exposing the whole Field (e.g., "This kitchen has 50 unused meals on Tuesday afternoons").
*   **`ReceiverCard`:** A validated, admitted entity with a matching need (e.g., an after-school program needing 50 meals on Tuesdays).
*   **`OverflowRail`:** The active logistical connection (the POWL v2 transition) that binds the `EdgeCard` to the `ReceiverCard`.

## 2. Mechanical Execution: Connecting the Edge

The connection of an Edge is not a database relationship. It is an **admitted transition** through the TrueX Consequence Membrane.

### Step 1: Field Discovery (Projection Surface)
Through a white-labeled projection surface (e.g., a Parish Dashboard), a steward identifies hidden capacity. They do not donate money; they declare an `EdgeCard`.
*   *Mechanism:* A Semantic Bit is flipped from `HIDDEN` to `UNHARVESTED`.

### Step 2: Receiver Matching (Compiled Cognition)
The system must find the correct Receiver. We do not use a raw LLM to guess the routing. 
*   *Mechanism:* Using HDIT (Hyperdimensional Information Theory) embedded in the branchless kernel, the `EdgeCard` vector is dot-product matched against the `ReceiverCard` vector to find the highest-admissibility routing path in milliseconds.

### Step 3: The Overflow Rail (Agentic Interoperability)
Once matched, an autonomous logistics agent is invoked to handle the physical or digital transfer.
*   *Mechanism:* An MCP-backed agent orchestrates the `OverflowRail`. This is modeled as a POWL v2 Partial Order graph. The agent must satisfy the preconditions of the Receiver (e.g., dietary restrictions, delivery window) before the TrueX kernel admits the transition.

### Step 4: The Stewardship Receipt (Operational Core)
The transfer is not complete when the goods leave the Field. It is complete when the consequence is realized.
*   *Mechanism:* When the Receiver confirms the provision, the TrueX kernel emits a BLAKE3-hashed **Stewardship Receipt** to the OpenTelemetry stream. 
*   *The Category Break:* A donation receipt says "50 meals left the kitchen." A Storehouse Stewardship receipt says "50 meals were consumed by the after-school program, yielding community fruit."

## 3. The 6-Week Gleaning Pilot (The Cell)

By connecting the edges, we run the first operational cell of the Storehouse Infrastructure.

*   **Week 1:** Teach the ontology (Field, Edge, Receiver, Storehouse).
*   **Week 2:** The community identifies one real `FieldCard` and exposes its `EdgeCard`.
*   **Week 3:** The TrueX kernel compiles the `OverflowRail` to a verified `ReceiverCard`.
*   **Week 4:** The provision moves across the rail.
*   **Week 5:** The system mints the `StewardshipReceipt`.
*   **Week 6:** The community reviews the receipt. The `FieldCard` expands because the overflow was routed to fruit rather than disposal.

## Conclusion
Connecting the edges means transforming wasted capacity into receipted provision. By treating gleaning not as a charity concept, but as a strict mechanical routing problem, TrueX allows communities to discover and utilize the fields they are currently missing.
